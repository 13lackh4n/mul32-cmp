Method: start at 5.000 ns; tighten (or loosen) in 0.5 ns steps until the pass/fail result flips (the coarse result is the last passing step), then bisect between the last pass and the first fail to <= 0.05 ns. Pass/fail is not perfectly monotonic in the period (place-and-route noise of a few tens of ps), so the achieved value is the tightest period that actually passed. Every point is listed in `reports/<module>_fmax_search.txt`.

## Conclusion

- **The behavioral baseline is the most area-efficient design.** It uses the fewest LUTs (48) and FFs (83) of all five, delivers one result per cycle at 197.5 MHz with a 2-cycle latency, and has the lowest dynamic energy per result (50 pJ at 100 MHz). Its price is 4 DSP48E1 slices. If DSPs are available, this is the design to use.
- **Karatsuba saves one DSP but costs fmax.** 3 DSPs instead of 4, paid for with 69 more LUTs, 149 more FFs and 51.4 MHz lower fmax (146.1 vs 197.5 MHz). The critical path is the 18-bit pre-add (a0+a1) feeding the middle DSP, whose multiply sits in the same cycle because at PIPE_STAGES=4 that DSP has no input or M register. **Future work:** PIPE_STAGES=5 adds a register after the multipliers so Vivado can use MREG; it is verified in simulation but has not been implemented, so its fmax gain is expected, not measured.
- **Wallace reaches the highest pipelined fmax despite the highest LUT count.** mult_wallace_b1 runs at 262.3 MHz (mult_wallace_b0: 244.3 MHz) with zero DSPs, but needs 1584/1259 LUTs (33x/26x the baseline) and 390/340 pJ per result. Booth radix-4 beats the plain tree on LUTs, FFs, fmax and power (-325 LUTs, -36 FFs, +18.0 MHz, -5 mW at 100 MHz; CARRY4 +1). The comparison with the baseline is not at equal latency: Wallace uses 4 pipeline stages, the baseline 2; a deeper behavioral pipeline was not implemented.
- **Shift-add's fmax is misleading.** Its 273.4 MHz is only the speed of one 33-bit add per cycle; with one result every 33 cycles its throughput is 8.29 M results/s, 24x lower than the baseline, and its energy per result (990 pJ) is the highest. Its only advantage is zero DSPs with few LUTs (105).

Overall: for a 32x32 multiplier on Artix-7, letting Vivado map `a * b` onto DSP48E1 is the best choice unless DSPs are the scarce resource; hand-built architectures win only on a specific axis (DSP count, raw clock rate) and pay for it elsewhere.
